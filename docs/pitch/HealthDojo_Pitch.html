The problem

About 1 in 3 joint-replacement patients falls within a year. Most falls happen at home.

~31%
of falls are environmental: a rug, a missing rail, a dark hallway.
−26%
fewer falls after fixing the home, and −38% in high-risk patients.
3.6×
higher odds of falling after knee replacement when the patient lives alone.

Home-health AI companies are shipping vision models into homes. Nobody can check whether they work. The clinical guidelines that define "safe" are PDFs, not tests. Real photos come with no answer key. And a systematic review found that environmental factors are largely missing from AI fall-risk models.

Sources: Rubenstein 2006, Age & Ageing (mean 31%, range 1–53%) · Clemson et al. 2023, Cochrane CD013258 (RaR 0.74; high-risk 0.62) · Alasbali et al. 2025, Cureus (OR 3.63) · Song et al. 2026, PMC12889826 (preprint)
Why now

From 2028, hospitals own the fall that happens at home on day 40.

CMS CJR-X

Joint replacement becomes a mandatory bundle nationwide

Starts 2028-01-01. 2,500+ hospitals. 90-day episodes. A post-discharge fall, readmission, or revision now comes out of the hospital's budget.

Models

Every vendor now ships a vision model

We scored 14 of them, 13 through a single Bedrock API. Plenty of models, and no clinical test for any of them.

Generation

Image editing is now precise enough to plant one hazard

A single edit changes about 2% of the pixels and leaves the rest of the room alone. That gives us controlled experiments at scale.

Hospitals will choose home-safety AI vendors. To choose, they need a benchmark built from the guidelines they already follow.

Does it know where to look?
